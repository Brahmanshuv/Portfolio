# 05-CAREER-INTELLIGENCE

## Purpose
Provides strategic career guidance, seniority leveling frameworks, portfolio interview walkthrough coaching, and deliberate practice loops for product designers aspiring to senior, lead, and principal positions.

## Use When
- The user explicitly asks for career advice, designer leveling benchmarks, or growth roadmaps.
- Preparing for portfolio presentations, design director interviews, or whiteboarding challenges.
- Structuring daily or weekly craft-improvement habits and skill-calibration routines.
- Evaluating job offers, positioning strategy, or professional development plans.

## Do Not Use For
- Routine portfolio coding, bug fixes, or CSS styling tasks.
- Active product design or UX flow wireframing.

## Designer Seniority Leveling Framework

```
JUNIOR DESIGNER       ──► MID-LEVEL DESIGNER       ──► SENIOR / LEAD DESIGNER
(Executes screens)        (Solves defined flows)       (Frames problems & systems)
```

| Dimension | Junior (L3) | Mid-Level (L4) | Senior (L5) | Staff / Principal (L6+) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operational Scope** | Individual UI tasks, screens, and components | End-to-end user flows and bounded features | Ambiguous problem spaces, cross-product systems | Company-wide product strategy, design systems, team multiplier |
| **Handling Ambiguity** | Needs explicit wireframes and detailed specs | Needs clear problem definition and user goals | Discovers and reframes problems from raw business signals | Anticipates industry shifts, creates new product categories |
| **Tradeoff Rigor** | Focuses only on visual aesthetics | Considers basic development feasibility | Balances speed, quality, debt, unit economics, and edge cases | Architects long-term platform scalability and organizational alignment |
| **Communication** | Explains visual choices ("I preferred this blue") | Explains user steps ("Users click here to...") | Explains business outcomes, tradeoffs, and system resilience | Synthesizes complex technical, commercial, and human dynamics |
| **Craft Standard** | Inconsistent spacing and unaligned typography | Clean components, standard Figma autolayout | Sophisticated typography, editorial rhythm, production parity | Sets the institutional visual and behavioral bar for the organization |

## The Portfolio Presentation & Interview Strategy
When presenting work to hiring managers, VP of Design, and cross-functional partners:

### 1. Narrative Arc for Case Study Presentations
1. **The Hook (60 seconds):** What was broken in the world, what was the commercial stake, and what did we achieve?
2. **The Constraints & Team (2 minutes):** Team composition, timeline, technical boundaries, and your specific ownership.
3. **The Core Insight / Turning Point (3 minutes):** The non-obvious realization that altered the product trajectory.
4. **Key Decisions & Tradeoffs (5 minutes):** Show 2–3 diverging concepts that were tested and rejected. Explain *why* they failed.
5. **The Final Solution & Walkthrough (5 minutes):** Walk through the live, interactive experience—focusing on edge cases, empty states, and system states.
6. **The Retrospective & Honest Reflection (2 minutes):** What would you do differently with today's knowledge? What remains unvalidated?

### 2. High-Impact Interview Behaviors
- **Own Your Boundaries:** Never claim sole credit for team work. Saying *"Engineering discovered a severe latency constraint, so I redesigned the interaction to be optimistic"* demonstrates real-world collaboration.
- **Answer with the Business Impact:** Connect visual changes to user comprehension, error reduction, or conversion.
- **Embrace Critique:** When interviewers probe a weak spot, do not get defensive. Engage: *"That is a valid tradeoff. In our testing we noticed X, but if Y was the primary metric, a split-view pattern would have been superior."*

## The Deliberate Practice Loop
To systematically ascend from Mid-Level to Senior/Lead craft:
1. **Daily Deconstruction (15 minutes):** Pick one elite digital product (Linear, Stripe, Apple HIG, Wise). Inspect a single flow. Document 3 subtle decisions that go unnoticed by untrained eyes.
2. **Micro-Prototyping (30 minutes):** Build one challenging interaction state in code or Figma (e.g., inline multi-cell table editing, gesture-driven bottom sheets, fluid clamp typography).
3. **Weekly Inversion Audit:** Take an existing design and ask: *"How does this fail?"* Design the recovery flow for offline states, extreme string lengths, and database timeouts.
