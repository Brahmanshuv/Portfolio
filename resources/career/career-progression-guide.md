# Product Designer Career Progression & Mindset Reference

## 1. The Designer Seniority Continuum

| Dimension | Junior Designer | Mid-Level Designer | Senior / Lead Product Designer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus** | Producing screens and following instructions | Solving well-defined flows and UI patterns | Defining problems, systems, tradeoffs, and product strategy |
| **Ambiguity** | Needs explicit wireframes and detailed specs | Needs clear problem statements and scopes | Thrives in ambiguous spaces, frames problems from raw signals |
| **Decision Making** | Based on personal preference or trend copying | Based on standard design patterns and heuristics | Based on business viability, user psychology, tech feasibility, and systems |
| **Tradeoff Awareness** | "Why can't we build everything?" | Aware of dev constraints during handoff | Proactively cuts scope, balances speed vs. quality, designs for edge cases |
| **Communication** | Explains visual choices ("I liked this blue") | Explains usability steps ("User clicks here to...") | Explains business outcomes, tradeoffs, and system resilience |
| **Craft** | Inconsistent spacing, unaligned typography | Clean components, standard Figma autolayout | Sophisticated typography, editorial rhythm, flawless states, production parity |

---

## 2. The Daily & Weekly Practice Loops
- **Daily Calibration (15 mins):** Inspect 1–2 world-class digital products or editorial websites. Analyze why their typography, layout, and pacing work. Deconstruct what was left out.
- **Micro-Execution (30 mins):** Rebuild a single complex interaction or state (e.g., an inline table edit, a multi-step filter, or a fluid responsive navbar) in Figma or code.
- **Weekly Systems Review:** Audit a past project for edge cases: What happens on offline mode? How does it look with German text (30% longer strings)? What does the empty state look like?

---

## 3. High-Value Mental Models for Designers
1. **First-Principles Thinking:** Strip away conventional UI patterns and ask: *"What is the core user job to be done, and what is the absolute simplest way to accomplish it?"*
2. **Second-Order Effects:** Every design decision creates ripple effects. Adding a new button solves one request but increases cognitive load for all future users and maintenance overhead for engineering.
3. **Inversion (Designing for Failure):** Instead of only designing the "happy path," start by designing the worst-case scenarios: network timeouts, permission denials, invalid inputs, and destructive actions.
