# Recruiter Psychology & Portfolio Hiring Speed Reference

## 1. The Reality of Portfolio Scans
Hiring managers, design directors, and recruiters review hundreds of portfolios in high-velocity hiring cycles.
- **Average initial review time:** **15 to 45 seconds**.
- **The Initial Question:** *"Is this person credible, relevant to my opening, and worth a 15-minute phone screen?"*
- **The Core Barrier:** Confusion, ambiguity, broken navigation, or generic AI templates cause immediate rejection.

---

## 2. The Three Reading Speeds

### Speed 1: The 8-Second Scan (First Impression & Positioning)
Within the first 8 seconds without scrolling:
1. **Identity:** Who are you? (Name and clear title/specialization).
2. **Value Proposition:** What kind of problems do you solve?
3. **Strongest Work:** Can I immediately see 1–2 high-caliber projects?
4. **Taste & Quality:** Does the typography, whitespace, and polish signal seniority?
5. **Next Action:** Is there an obvious way to contact, view resume, or explore a project?

### Speed 2: The 60-Second Skim (Project & Case Study Skim)
When a reviewer clicks into a featured project:
1. **One-Screen Brief:** Problem, role, scope, platform, and timeline.
2. **Visual Proof:** Real, high-fidelity UI screens and prototypes (not just sticky notes or whiteboards).
3. **Scannable Section Heads:** Clear editorial titles communicating decisions (e.g., *"Selection should feel obvious before movement feels fast"* rather than generic *"Research Phase"*).
4. **Key Decisions:** Why did the design take this direction over alternatives?

### Speed 3: The Deep Read (Hiring Manager / Design Director Evaluation)
If selected for the interview loop, the team performs a deep audit:
1. **Systems Thinking:** Edge cases, states (loading, empty, error, recovery), scalable component architecture.
2. **Business & User Impact:** Did the designer understand commercial reality and constraints?
3. **Tradeoffs:** What was sacrificed and why?
4. **Truth & Honesty:** Did the designer honestly label assumptions and team contributions, or claim sole ownership of everything?

---

## 3. High-Risk Red Flags That Disqualify Candidates
1. **UX Theatre:** Giant double-diamond diagrams, textbook persona sheets with fake hobbies ("Sarah loves coffee and dogs"), and walls of Post-it notes with zero connection to the final UI.
2. **Fabricated Metrics:** Claiming "+340% conversion rate" or "$12M revenue increase" on conceptual, student, or unlaunched projects.
3. **Broken Navigation / Technical Friction:** Sluggish scroll animations, broken mobile responsive layouts, unclickable work cards, missing project URLs.
4. **Generic AI Aesthetics:** Unrestrained glassmorphism, floating purple glow cards, endless pill badges, and generic dashboard mockups that look identical to every template on Dribbble.
5. **Lack of Finished Craft:** Great research followed by amateur visual typography, sloppy alignment, and inconsistent spacing.
